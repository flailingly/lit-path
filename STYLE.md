# Style guide

How Scout's Folio should look, sound and feel. When something new doesn't fit these rules, change the new thing, not the rules, unless the owner says otherwise.

## The feel in one line

A real map of San Francisco with a quiet magical city showing through the fog: grounded, optimistic, a little uncanny, written for adults.

## Look

- **The map must be obviously real SF.** Real OpenStreetMap streets and labels, drawn chunky (tiles one zoom level out, scaled up without smoothing). Street names stay readable.
- **The fog looks like fog.** Grey-white, drifting, pixelated, soft-edged. Walking into it clears it; it creeps back over days. Never a flat color or a hatch pattern.
- **Pixel art, point-and-click era.** Think LucasArts adventure games: small detailed sprites, hard 1-pixel drop shadows, square corners. Sprites are 9x9 (places) or 8x12 (the Scout) at 2 to 3 px per pixel.
- **Dialogue boxes are dark.** Toasts, the Visit button and map labels use dark navy boxes (`#1d1a2b`) with paper or gold pixel text.
- **The Folio pages are parchment.** Sheets (Dispatch, Logbook, Satchel) are warm paper with ink text, so the map is the world and the sheets are your notebook.

| Role | Color |
|---|---|

Pickup colors: Flora `#7d9a5a`, Provisions `#d9a43a`, Knowledge `#5d6fa8`, Sea finds `#4f8fa0`, Sky finds `#8fa9c4`, Pigment `#c0607a`.

### Type

- **Pixelify Sans** for headings, buttons, numbers and map labels.
- **IM Fell English** for spoken lines and lore: anything a character says or the city remembers.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible** for everything you need to read quickly, like instructions, settings and stats.

## Voice

**Grounded urban fantasy for adults.** Characters are people (or animals) with a job, a history and one strange detail. The magic is stated plainly, never explained or exclaimed.

- **Do:** "Holloway swears the copper turns greener whenever someone learns something here."
- **Do:** "Gus says the cable hums a different note when the fog is coming."
- **Don't:** talking objects as mascots ("Graffito, a chatty paint can"), cutesy names, exclamation points, "magical!", baby talk.
- **Don't:** grimdark. Nothing is dying, cursed or at war. The Fading is quiet, not evil.

**Every place gets one real fact and one uncanny line.** Double-check real facts. The uncanny line should be specific and small.

**Recovery is part of life, not the plot.** The game knows about the knee (Oriel the physio, rest days, check-ins) and is kind about it. It never guilts, never says "push through", and treats rest as a valid move.

**Interface text is plain.** Buttons say what happens ("Set out", "End outing", "Copy save code"). Errors say what went wrong and what to do. Flavor belongs in characters' mouths, not in buttons.

## Setting quick reference

- **The Wayfarer's Guild:** walkers and mapmakers. The player is a Scout.
- **The Fading:** places nobody notices go quiet under the fog. Locals call it Karl.
- **The Shimmer and thresholds:** where the hidden city shows through. They feel like a temperature drop or a radio losing its station.
- **The Ley-Grid:** old lines under Muni tracks, cable slots and buried creeks.
- **The factions:**
  - **Hearthkeepers:** every real café and bakery.
  - **Track-Keepers:** transit and instrument people.
  - **The Courier Union:** runners who carry things they won't name.
- **The cast:**
  - **Maren:** runs dispatch.
  - **Kip:** a courier with a brass magpie.
  - **Professor Val:** keeps the Guild archives.
  - **Bramble:** the cat who points out thresholds.
  - **Oriel:** the Guild's physio.
